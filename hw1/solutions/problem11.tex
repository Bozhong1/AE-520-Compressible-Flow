\section*{Problem 11 --- Solution}
Subscripts 1 and 2 denote methane and argon, respectively; subscript 0 denotes
an initial state. Treat both gases as ideal and calorically perfect, with the
prescribed degrees of freedom. The outer vessel is rigid and insulated, with
no shaft work or changes in endpoint bulk kinetic or potential energy.
Neglect the partition's volume and heat capacity. Here $E$ and $S$ are totals,
$V$ is volume, and $R_u=8.3144626~\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

\partlabel{(a)}
The partition is fixed: $V_{i,f}=V_{i,0}$. For the combined system,
$Q_{\mathrm{ext}}=W_{\mathrm{ext}}=0$.
% L1 notes PDF p.2: molar mass/basis definitions.
% L2 notes PDF p.5 (printed p.20), Eq. (39): cv=(f/2)R.
% Multiplication by molar mass gives cv_bar=(f/2)Ru.
First calculate the amounts and the total heat capacities $C_i$ of the gas samples:
\begin{align*}
n_1&=\frac{m_1}{M_1}=\frac{1}{0.0160425}=62.334424~\mathrm{mol},
&n_2&=\frac{1}{0.039948}=25.032542~\mathrm{mol},\\
\bar c_{v,i}&=\frac{f_i}{2}R_u,
&C_i&=n_i\bar c_{v,i}=m_ic_{v,i},\\
C_1&=n_1(3R_u)=1554.831716~\mathrm{J/K},
&C_2&=n_2\left(\frac32R_u\right)=312.198205~\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{align*}
% L1 notes PDF p.13, Eqs. (21), (24): closed-system first law.
% L3 notes PDF p.1 (printed p.25), Eq. (74): CPG internal energy.
The first law gives
\begin{align*}
\Delta E_1+\Delta E_2&=0,\\
C_1(T_{f,a}-300)+C_2(T_{f,a}-600)&=0,\\
T_{f,a}&=\frac{C_1(300)+C_2(600)}{C_1+C_2}
=\boxed{350.164949~\mathrm{K}}.
\end{align*}
% L2 notes PDF p.9 (printed p.24), Eq. (69), multiplied by each gas mass.
% Constant species mass converts the specific-volume ratio into a total-volume ratio.
For each gas, the entropy change is
\[
\Delta S_i=C_i\ln\frac{T_{i,f}}{T_{i,0}}
+n_iR_u\ln\frac{V_{i,f}}{V_{i,0}}.
\]
Since the compartment volumes are unchanged, $\ln(V_{i,f}/V_{i,0})=0$:
\begin{align*}
\Delta S_{1,a}&=1554.831716\ln\frac{350.164949}{300}
=\boxed{+240.411~\mathrm{J/K}},\\
\Delta S_{2,a}&=312.198205\ln\frac{350.164949}{600}
=\boxed{-168.127~\mathrm{J/K}},\\
\Delta S_a&=\Delta S_{1,a}+\Delta S_{2,a}
=\boxed{72.2843~\mathrm{J/K}}>0.
\end{align*}
% L2 notes PDF p.2 (printed p.17), Eqs. (25)--(26): isolated-system entropy balance.
The system is isolated, so $S_{\mathrm{gen},a}=\Delta S_a$.
Heat transfer across a finite temperature difference is irreversible.
The final temperature is closer to $300~\mathrm{K}$ because methane has the larger total heat capacity.

% L3 notes PDF p.1, Eq. (72): ideal-gas equation of state, applied at fixed n,V.
The fixed partition can support unequal final pressures:
\[
p_{i,a}=p_{i,0}\frac{T_{f,a}}{T_{i,0}},\qquad
p_{1,a}=1.16721650~\mathrm{bar},\qquad
p_{2,a}=0.58360825~\mathrm{bar}.
\]

\newpage
\partlabel{(b)}
Return to the original initial states. The ideal-gas internal energies depend
only on temperature, so removing the partition adds no energy-of-mixing term:
% Same first law and CPG energy relation as (a); species internal energies are additive.
\[
C_1(T_{f,b}-300)+C_2(T_{f,b}-600)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{T_{f,b}=T_{f,a}=350.164949~\mathrm{K}}.
\]
% L3 notes PDF p.1, Eq. (72), converted to pV=nRuT; sum volumes for the rigid vessel.
From the initial ideal-gas states,
\begin{align*}
V_{i,0}&=\frac{n_iR_uT_{i,0}}{p_{i,0}},\qquad p_{i,0}=10^5~\mathrm{Pa},\\
V_{1,0}&=1.554831716~\mathrm{m^3},\qquad
V_{2,0}=1.248792821~\mathrm{m^3},\\
V&=V_{1,0}+V_{2,0}=2.803624536~\mathrm{m^3}.
\end{align*}
The final mixture pressure is
\[
p_f=\frac{(n_1+n_2)R_uT_{f,b}}{V}
=90726.52~\mathrm{Pa}
=\boxed{0.907265~\mathrm{bar}}.
\]
% L2 notes PDF p.9, Eq. (69) + L4 notes PDF p.5 (printed p.46), Eq. (203).
% Each ideal-gas species has access to the entire mixture volume V.
Each species now occupies the entire vessel, so
\[
\Delta S_{i,b}
=C_i\ln\frac{T_{f,b}}{T_{i,0}}+n_iR_u\ln\frac{V}{V_{i,0}}
=\Delta S_{i,a}+n_iR_u\ln\frac{V}{V_{i,0}}.
\]
Therefore,
\begin{align*}
\Delta S_{1,b}
&=240.410961+(62.334424)(8.3144626)\ln\frac{2.803624536}{1.554831716}
=\boxed{545.959~\mathrm{J/K}},\\
\Delta S_{2,b}
&=-168.126641+(25.032542)(8.3144626)\ln\frac{2.803624536}{1.248792821}
=\boxed{+0.197252~\mathrm{J/K}},\\
\Delta S_b&=\Delta S_{1,b}+\Delta S_{2,b}
=\boxed{546.156~\mathrm{J/K}}=S_{\mathrm{gen},b}>0.
\end{align*}
Unrounded values are retained: argon's cooling and volume terms nearly cancel,
but its net entropy change is positive.

\partlabel{(c)}
% Subtract the total entropy changes obtained in (a) and (b).
The additional entropy is
\[
\Delta S_{\mathrm{extra}}=\Delta S_b-\Delta S_a
\simeq 546.156-72.2843
=\boxed{473.872~\mathrm{J/K}}.
\]
In (b), each gas can spread through the entire vessel, whereas in (a) it stays
in its original compartment. I think this additional volume contribution is
related to ideal-gas mixing. I have not yet worked out how to connect it to the
usual mole-fraction expression for mixing entropy.
