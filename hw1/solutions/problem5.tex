\section*{Problem 5 --- Solution}

Let $h$ and $c$ denote the initially hot and cold blocks. Each rigid block has
constant total heat capacity $C=500\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The combined system is
isolated; kinetic and potential energy changes are negligible.

\partlabel{(a)}
% First law: Lecture 1 notes, PDF p.13 (printed p.16), Eqs. (21), (24).
% dE=C dT: constant total block heat capacity specified in Problem 5.
For the two blocks together, $Q_{\mathrm{ext}}=W_{\mathrm{ext}}=0$. Hence
\begin{align*}
\Delta E_h+\Delta E_c&=0,\\
C(T_f-500\,\mathrm{K})+C(T_f-300\,\mathrm{K})&=0,\\
T_f&=\boxed{400\,\mathrm{K}}.
\end{align*}
The hot block loses $50.0\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and the cold block gains the same amount.

\partlabel{(b)}
% Entropy balance: Lecture 2 notes, PDF p.2 (printed p.17), Eqs. (25)-(26).
% Reversible comparison path: Lecture 2 notes, PDF p.3 (printed p.18),
% Eq. (31) and the following point 1. Entropy is a state property.
The actual heat transfer is irreversible. Since entropy is a state property,
use a reversible auxiliary path between each block's initial and final states:
\[
\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}=dE=C\,dT,
\qquad
\Delta S=\int_{T_i}^{T_f}\frac{\delta Q_{\mathrm{rev}}}{T}
=C\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i}.
\]
Thus
\begin{align*}
\Delta S_h&=(500\,\mathrm{J/K})\ln\frac{400}{500}
=\boxed{-111.57\,\mathrm{J/K}},\\
\Delta S_c&=(500\,\mathrm{J/K})\ln\frac{400}{300}
=\boxed{+143.84\,\mathrm{J/K}}.
\end{align*}
The external surroundings are unchanged, so
\[
\boxed{\Delta S_{\mathrm{universe}}
=\Delta S_h+\Delta S_c
=32.27\,\mathrm{J/K}>0.}
\]
This is also the entropy generated within the isolated two-block system.

\partlabel{(c)}
The first law requires only that energy lost by one block equal energy gained
by the other; it permits either sign of heat transfer. The second law selects
the spontaneous direction. In a uniform-temperature description of each block,
let $\delta Q>0$ flow from the hot block to the cold block. Then
% Combine dS=dE/T for each rigid equilibrium block with the isolated-system
% entropy inequality: Lecture 2 notes, PDF p.2 (printed p.17), Eq. (26).
\[
dS_{\mathrm{universe}}
=-\frac{\delta Q}{T_h}+\frac{\delta Q}{T_c}
=\delta Q\left(\frac{1}{T_c}-\frac{1}{T_h}\right)>0
\qquad (T_h>T_c).
\]
Heat flow in the reverse direction, with no other effect, would decrease the
universe's entropy and violate the second law.
