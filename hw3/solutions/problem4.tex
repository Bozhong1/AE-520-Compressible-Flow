\section*{Problem 4}

Steady flow with fixed captured mass flow and upstream stagnation state;
the shock is adiabatic, and all other unheated sections are isentropic.
Interpret the heating immediately upstream of the second throat as a short
constant-area section at $A_{t,2}$, initially entered at $M=0.80$; the diagram's
$M=0.40$ station is in the wider diffuser.
$q>0$ is heat added per unit gas mass, and $c_p=1004.5~\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$.

% HW3 PDF p.3: incomplete introductory sentence; diagram identifies the stations.
% L12 notes PDF p.1, Eq. (228). Unlike L13 Eq. (304), this first throat is not sonic.
Let $A_u^*$ and $A_d^*$ be the sonic reference areas before and after the shock,
with $A_d^*$ evaluated before heating. The first physical throat is not sonic:
\[
\frac{A_{t,1}}{A_u^*}=F(1.20)=1.03043975>1,
\qquad A_{t,1}\ne A_u^*.
\]

\partlabel{(a)}
% L10 notes PDF p.2, Eqs. (47)--(49): Rayleigh balances.
% PDF p.8, Eq. (104), and p.9, Eq. (108): sonic temperature and heat.
For constant-area heating with negligible friction,
\[
\rho u=\text{constant},\qquad p+\rho u^2=\text{constant},\qquad
q=h_{0,\mathrm{out}}-h_{0,\mathrm{in}}
=c_p(T_{0,\mathrm{out}}-T_{0,\mathrm{in}}).
\]
The shock and unheated sections preserve $T_0$, so
$T_{0,\mathrm{in}}=700~\mathrm K$. At thermal choking, $M_{\mathrm{out}}=1$:
\begin{align*}
\frac{T_{0,\mathrm{in}}}{T_0^*}
&=\frac{2(\gamma+1)M_{\mathrm{in}}^2
\left[1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_{\mathrm{in}}^2\right]}
{(1+\gamma M_{\mathrm{in}}^2)^2}\\
&=\frac{2(2.4)(0.80)^2[1+0.2(0.80)^2]}
{[1+1.4(0.80)^2]^2}=0.963948085,\\
T_0^*&=\frac{700}{0.963948085}=726.180186~\mathrm K,\\
q_a&=1004.5(726.180186-700)=26297.997~\mathrm{J/kg}
=\boxed{26.2980~\mathrm{kJ/kg}}.
\end{align*}
Here $T_0^*$ belongs to the sonic state on this Rayleigh line.

\partlabel{(b)}
% L12 notes PDF p.1, Eq. (228); L13 PDF p.6, Eqs. (315)--(319).
With no heating and the original shock fixed, $A_d^*$ is unchanged.
Write $A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}$ for the original second-throat area:
\begin{gather*}
A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}=F(0.80)A_d^*,\qquad F(0.80)=1.03823000,\\
A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{choked}}=A_d^*
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{choked}}}{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}}
=\frac{1}{F(0.80)}=0.963177716,\\
\boxed{\frac{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}-A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{choked}}}
{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}}=1-0.963177716=3.68223\%.}
\end{gather*}

\partlabel{(c)}
% L9 notes PDF p.4, Eq. (27): Pi(M).
% L13 notes PDF p.6, Eq. (311): downstream critical area.
% PDF pp.7--8, Eqs. (330)--(333): shock adjustment after thermal choking.
Use the shock pressure-recovery function $\Pi(M)$ defined in Problem 2:
\[
\Pi(1.50)=0.929786512,\qquad \Pi(1.20)=0.992798398.
\]
For fixed $\dot m$ and the unchanged pre-heating $T_0$, $A_d^*=A_u^*/\Pi$.
The subsonic area--Mach root below is found by bisection
on $[10^{-7},1]$:
\begin{align*}
A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}
&=F(0.80)\frac{A_u^*}{\Pi(1.50)},\qquad
A_{d,\mathrm{lim}}^*=\frac{A_u^*}{\Pi(1.20)},\\
F(M_{\mathrm{pre}})
&=\frac{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}}{A_{d,\mathrm{lim}}^*}
=F(0.80)\frac{\Pi(1.20)}{\Pi(1.50)}\\
&=(1.03823000)\frac{0.992798398}{0.929786512}=1.10859113,\\
M_{\mathrm{pre}}<1&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{M_{\mathrm{pre}}=0.681329}.
\end{align*}
% L10 notes PDF p.8, Eq. (104), and p.9, Eq. (108).
Apply the Rayleigh relation in (a) to this new pre-heating state:
\begin{align*}
\frac{700}{T_{0,\mathrm{lim}}^*}
&=\frac{2(2.4)(0.681328779)^2[1+0.2(0.681328779)^2]}
{[1+1.4(0.681328779)^2]^2}=0.894541958,\\
T_{0,\mathrm{lim}}^*&=\frac{700}{0.894541958}=782.523384~\mathrm K,\\
q_{\max}&=1004.5(782.523384-700)=82894.739~\mathrm{J/kg}
=\boxed{82.8947~\mathrm{kJ/kg}}.
\end{align*}
This is the total heat from the unheated state. The additional heat beyond (a)
is $q_{\max}-q_a=56.5967~\mathrm{kJ/kg}$.
The weaker shock preserves more stagnation pressure, reducing the pre-heating
Mach number at the fixed second-throat area and increasing the heat addition
compatible with a sonic second throat.

\partlabel{(d)}
% L12 notes PDF p.1, Eq. (228), and PDF p.4, Eq. (244).
% Combine the original geometry with the weaker-shock critical area, without heating.
With $q=0$, choking at the limiting shock position requires
$A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{lim}}=A_{d,\mathrm{lim}}^*$:
\begin{align*}
\frac{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{lim}}}{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}}
&=\frac{A_u^*/\Pi(1.20)}{F(0.80)A_u^*/\Pi(1.50)}
=\frac{\Pi(1.50)}{\Pi(1.20)F(0.80)}\\
&=\frac{0.929786512}{(0.992798398)(1.03823000)}=0.902045824,\\
\frac{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}-A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{lim}}}
{A_{t,2}^{\mathrm{orig}}}
&=1-0.902045824=\boxed{9.79542\%}.
\end{align*}
This total reduction is relative to the original geometry. It exceeds the
$3.68223\%$ required for first choking because the further reduction moves
the shock upstream to the first constriction.
