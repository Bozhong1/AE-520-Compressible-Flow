\question Two routes to the same final pressure (sample only).
One mole of ideal oxygen starts at $T_1=300~\mathrm{K}$ and
$p_1=1~\mathrm{bar}$. The specified final pressure is $p_2=8~\mathrm{bar}$.
Take $\gamma=1.40$ and assume a calorically perfect gas.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Sample calculation: the adiabatic work relation.}
For a closed system with negligible changes in bulk kinetic and potential
energy, heat into the gas is positive and work done by the gas is positive.
The first law is
\begin{equation*}
  dE=\delta Q-\delta W_{\mathrm{by}}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Delta E=Q-W_{\mathrm{by}}.
\end{equation*}
\noindent\textit{Source:} Lecture 1 organized notes, PDF page 13
(printed page 16), Eqs.~(21)--(24). The finite-process form follows by integration.
Here $E$ denotes the total internal energy of the gas.

\medskip
For an adiabatic process, $Q=0$. Rearranging the first law therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta E=-W_{\mathrm{by,ad}}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  W_{\mathrm{by,ad}}=-\Delta E.
\end{equation*}
For a calorically perfect gas, subtract the endpoint values in
$e=c_vT+e_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and multiply by the fixed mass $m$:
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta E=m c_v(T_2-T_1)=n\bar c_v(T_2-T_1).
\end{equation*}
\noindent\textit{Source:} Lecture 3 organized notes, PDF page 1
(printed page 25), Eq.~(74), followed by endpoint subtraction and a basis
conversion. Here $e$ and $c_v$ are mass-specific, $n$ is the amount in moles,
and $\bar c_v$ is the molar heat capacity; by definition $m c_v=n\bar c_v$.

\medskip
Substituting this energy change into the adiabatic first law gives
\begin{equation*}
  \boxed{W_{\mathrm{by,ad}}=-n\bar c_v(T_2-T_1).}
\end{equation*}
This is a combination of the two cited relations. It is not a separate
formula quoted directly from the notes. If compression raises the temperature,
work done by the gas is negative.
